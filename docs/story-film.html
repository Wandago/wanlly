Wanlly · a case study

What would it take to give every student frontier AI, for free?

Nairobi · 2026
Chapter one · The cost of access
$20a month: the going rate for one frontier AI subscription

In Nairobi that is about KSh 2,600, roughly two weeks of campus lunches. In Lagos, about ₦26,000. In Mumbai, about ₹1,900.

The best tools are priced in dollars, on cards most students don’t have.

Approximate, October 2026 exchange rates
The insight

Students already pay for the internet with their attention.

So let attention pay for intelligence: sponsors fund the AI, and people choose when to see them.

Chapter two · The model

Every credit is backed by a sponsor.

SponsorPays for attentionA short ad, or a task: try an app, answer a survey, take a free course.
CreditsLand with the studentFour for a finished ad. One credit covers half a US cent of model cost.
ModelsDo the workChat, code and design on Claude, Gemini and open models.
No card. No subscription. Usage limits are the same for everyone.
Chapter three · The product

One workspace, from first question to shipped project.

ChatCoursework, CVs, business plans, in plain language.
CodeDescribe an app; watch it write real files and push them to GitHub.
DesignPosters, pitch decks and landing pages, editable.

Claude Haiku · Sonnet · Opus  /  Gemini Flash  /  free open models

Chapter four · Principles

Paid for by sponsors. Never shaped by them.

  • 01Ads never appear inside an AI answer.
  • 02Sponsors never see prompts, chats or work.
  • 03No ads on health, mental health or politics.
  • 04No gambling, no get-rich-quick, no essay mills.
Chapter five · Where it goes next

Built in Nairobi. Opening to students and creators across Africa and South Asia.

Now in beta. Brands that want to reach the next generation of builders can run campaigns themselves, at a price they set.

Good ideas shouldn’t wait for a credit card.
